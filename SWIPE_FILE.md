# Swipe file — hooks that survived the loop

## Twilight Shroud (1-cost hexproof spell) — "the removal he can't cast"
Goal: honor Alex's real thesis (NOT broken, NOT overrated — genuinely great) without
bait. Survived 5 adversarial review passes. Big lesson the loop taught: the real insight
(the card's value is invisible/held-threat, and THAT is why the community can't agree) is
the PAYOFF, not the opener — open on the concrete board moment that shows it, don't open
on the abstraction or the discourse.

- **Spoken hook (frame 1):** "My opponent has the removal to win this game. He knows I'm
  holding Twilight Shroud — and that's the exact reason he can't cast it."
- **First frame (visual):** live gameplay — opponent's cursor hovers over the lethal
  removal aimed at your unit, holds, pulls back. Your hand shows Twilight Shroud (known,
  not a withhold). Tension = his hesitation. NO fake "grays out" overlay — the card does
  nothing until cast; the freeze lives in his head, and that's the honest point.
- **On-screen title (distinct from spoken):** "He knows what I have. He still can't cast it."
- **Ending hook (reframe):** "He never cast it. That removal won't show up on a tier list
  or a highlight reel — and that's the exact reason half of you call this card broken,
  half call it a trap, and you're both wrong."
- **Load-bearing:** the un-closeable loop is "a KNOWN card still freezes lethal removal —
  how?" A player can't answer it in his head, because knowing the card is the whole point
  and it still doesn't help the opponent. Body MUST show one concrete turn where the read
  changed his line, or the ending collapses to a 'skill issue' tautology.
- **Open objection (uncapped):** last reviewer's fix (make the READ the mystery, card
  known, no gray-out) was applied but not re-reviewed. Also: keep the stakes SPECIFIC
  (life totals, what dies) — generic "my best unit" is scroll-tolerable.

## Recurring weakness to watch
Alex's instinct toward yes/no bait titles ("Is this broken?"). A hook the viewer can
answer in his own head is dead — he answers and scrolls. Convert the honest take into a
claim only the video can resolve.
