## [DOCUMENTED] — traceable to Oren
1. **Every hook has a visual AND an audio component.** His most-cited hook line. Don't
   write a spoken hook and call it done — the first *frame* is half the hook.
   Src: x.com/orenmeetsworld/status/1808175116142236153 (2024-07-01)
2. **The hook must matter to the audience.** Same tweet: "the hook needs to matter to
   audience." Attention alone isn't enough — it has to be about something the viewer
   cares about (here: their own play/craft decision).
3. **The "ending hook" — add a second hook.** Same tweet names an "ending hook" concept:
   a second hook (near the end). Exact payoff wording truncated in source; the "drives
   rewatch" read is plausible but [UNVERIFIED] as his phrasing.
4. **Hook = a set of teachable tactics, not a buzzword.** He has a dedicated hook-tactics
   video (tactics themselves paywalled/unreached).
5. **Build a WIDE list of hooks, then cut down.** From his paid masterclass description:
   generate ~30–40 hook/concept options per session and test many (different settings,
   creators, hooks) rather than trusting one. Volume + testing beats one magic line.
6. **(2026) Hook still reigns, but recognition + visible effort increasingly do the
   scroll-stop, and value must be wrapped in story, not delivered bare.**
   Src: hyperstudios.us/p/the-state-of-content-2026 ; youtube.com/watch?v=uy7f44INEOw

## [MY READ] — my working extensions (reason, not gospel)
- A hook the viewer can *answer in their own head* is dead — they close the loop and
  scroll. Yes/no bait ("Is X broken?") is the classic version: most people answer it and
  leave. Make a claim only the video can resolve.
- When the creator's genuine take is more interesting than the bait, the honest take IS
  the stronger hook — and it protects retention, because a bait hook whose payoff
  contradicts it feels like a switch and tanks watch-through (ties to #2 + #6).
